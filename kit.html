The job-search starter kit

Five things you can do this week. Each takes under an hour. Do one tonight, not all five.

1Fix your LinkedIn headline

"Student at Utah State University" is the default. It says nothing. Say what you do, for whom, and one proof point.

Formula [What you do] for [who] | [one proof point] | USU [major], [year]
Example (swap in your own facts) Marketing student helping local shops get found online | Ran a $50 ad test for a campus club | USU Business, junior

2Rewrite one resume bullet with a number

Employers skim. A number and a result stop the skim.

Formula Did [what], using [how], which led to [result with a number]
Before: Member of the marketing club After: Grew club Instagram from 200 to 450 followers in 8 weeks by posting three Reels a week

No numbers yet? That's what step 4 is for.

3Send three short messages

Find USU alumni on LinkedIn (search your school, filter by company or job title you want). Ask for advice, not a job. Follow up once after five days.

Hi [Name], I'm a [year] at USU studying [major]. I saw you work at [company] as [role]. I'm trying to learn how people get into [field]. Could I ask you two quick questions by message, or take 15 minutes on a call? Thanks either way. - [Your name]

4Make one proof piece

Pick a campus club, a local shop, or your own side project. One goal, one small test, one page.

Goal: What I tried: Result (with numbers): What I learned: What I'd do next:

Post it on LinkedIn and link it in your headline.

5Run a weekly loop (juniors and seniors)

  • 5 applications and 3 outreach messages every week
  • Track them in a sheet: company, role, date, contact, status, next step
  • Tailor the top three resume bullets to each posting
  • Every Friday, look at what got replies and change one thing. Same loop as the live build.

Freshmen and sophomores: swap this for "join one club where you run something, and start your proof piece."
